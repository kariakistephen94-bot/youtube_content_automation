Your goalkeeper gets injured. Why does a teammate have to leave the pitch?

That is the unusual twist in a Premier League trial this season.

According to the league, when play stops for a goalkeeper injury, the manager must nominate an outfield player to leave immediately.

That player stays off for at least one minute after play restarts.

The point? Discourage stoppages being used to break momentum or turn an injury break into a tactical meeting.

So the keeper receives treatment, but the team temporarily loses another player.

My concern is the trade-off: genuine injuries can happen too.

Would you keep this trial, or tackle the delays another way?
